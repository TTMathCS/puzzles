# Puzzles, and why they work

A good puzzle is a small world with exact rules. You can hold all of it in your head, and yet the answer hides.
This site collects the classics: logic puzzles you solve by pure reasoning, math puzzles you can play right here on
the page, and the big ideas behind them. Every page has a puzzle to try first, then the thinking that cracks it, and
then, in closed boxes you can open if you like, the deeper math.

The picture above holds three answers from the site, worked out by a computer program rather than drawn by hand:
eight queens that do not attack each other, a Tower of Hanoi part-way through the fastest solution, and the oldest
magic square, where every row, column and diagonal adds to 15. They stand on a Martian evening, with the blue sunset
Mars really has.

## How the pages work

Each page is a five to six minute read. A badge under the title says who it is for: **Beginner** pages need nothing
but curiosity, **Intermediate** pages use school math, and **Expert** pages reach the research. Answers are always in
closed boxes, so nothing is spoiled until you choose. The sources for each page are listed, closed, at its end.
Everything here, the text, the puzzles and the pictures, is AI-created; see [About](about.md).

This is a sister site of [Algorithms](https://cs.akadian.ca/), [Math](https://math.akadian.ca/) and
[Cryptography](https://cryptography.akadian.ca/), and part of [Mars – your new home](https://www.akadian.ca/).
