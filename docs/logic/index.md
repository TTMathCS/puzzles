# Logic puzzles

*Puzzles solved by reasoning alone: no guessing, no arithmetic beyond counting*

A logic puzzle gives you a few facts and asks what must follow. The skill it trains is the one a detective, a
scientist and a programmer share: hold several possibilities in mind, follow each one until it breaks, and keep only
what survives. Each page here starts with a puzzle, then shows the reasoning, then opens into the history and the
mathematics.

## Three habits that solve most logic puzzles

**Suppose, then test.** Pick one possibility ("suppose A is a knight") and follow it. If it leads to something
impossible, the supposition was wrong. This is *proof by contradiction*, the oldest tool in mathematics.

**Write the states down.** A puzzle that seems to need cleverness often needs only bookkeeping: a grid of who could be
where, or a list of every position the puzzle can be in. Once the possibilities are on paper, the answer often
appears by itself.

**Look for what is forced.** In Sudoku, a square where only one digit fits; in the zebra puzzle, a clue that leaves a
single house. Fill in what is forced, and new forced moves appear. Most published puzzles can be solved this way from
start to finish, with no guessing at all.
